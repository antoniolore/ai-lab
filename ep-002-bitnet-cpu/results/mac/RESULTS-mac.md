Reading [measured, this session; interpretation I]:
- Against FP16 — the paper's baseline — bitnet.cpp is ~2.5–2.8x faster at decode on this CPU. The paper's ARM figure for a 2.5B dummy is 2.42x (M2 Ultra, Table 4): same order.
- **That gain is the ternary weights, not the engine:** stock llama.cpp with its own TQ2_0 type gets the same ~2.8x over F16 and is within −15 %/+13 % of bitnet.cpp. With F16 embeddings on both sides (like for like) the difference is 1–13 %.
- Against what a llama.cpp user would actually download (Qwen2.5-1.5B Q4_K_M, quality comparable per Microsoft's own card), bitnet.cpp 2B4T is **slower** at decode (0.92x at 12 threads, 0.61x at 2).
- H1 (2.5–4x vs F16), H2 (TQ2_0 within ±20 %) and H3 (≤ 1.5x vs Q4_K_M) hold on the Mac. H5 (ratios shrink on the M4) not confirmed: ratios are similar to the paper's for this size.

## Prefill (pp512)

bitnet.cpp `404980e` prefill is 395 t/s at 12 threads (same as stock F16 and TQ2_0, ~400) and 75 t/s at 2 threads, where stock rows stay at ~380–400: at 2 threads the stock F16/TQ2_0/TQ1_0 prefill is almost thread-independent, consistent with Accelerate doing the matmuls [I]. Stock Q4_0 and Qwen Q4_K_M prefill is faster than bitnet.cpp at 12 threads (724 and 813 t/s). Prefill on the Mac is therefore not a like-for-like CPU-core comparison and is not used for the verdict.
